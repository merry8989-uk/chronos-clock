Android · Clock & alarms

Chronos

A clock that actually wakes you: exact alarms, wake-up missions you have to finish before the alarm stops, sleep and snoring reports, your own photo behind the ring screen — plus a world clock, timer and lap stopwatch. No ads, no account, no cloud.

Current version 3.0.0 · Package com.osintgram.chronos · Requires Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer

Install

One-tap install from inside the app

Open Settings → About → Check for updates. Chronos asks GitHub for the newest release, downloads the signed APK and hands it to the Android installer, so the update installs straight over the version you already have. No browser involved.

First install

Download the APK from the releases page, open it, and allow "install unknown apps" for your file manager when Android asks. Because the package ID and signing key never change, every future release installs as a normal update.

Alarms

  • Exact scheduling — alarms use AlarmManager's alarm-clock API, so they fire on the minute, and the system shows its alarm icon.
  • Full-screen ring screen — turns the screen on and shows over the lock screen, with Snooze and Dismiss.
  • Repeat patterns — once, every day, weekdays, weekends, or any custom set of days.
  • Skip public holidays — fixed Indian national holidays are built in (Republic Day, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti) and you can add any date of your own, such as the festivals that move each year.
  • Per-alarm sound — pick any alarm tone already on your device, or keep the system default.
  • Gradual volume — the tone ramps up from quiet instead of jolting you awake.
  • Snooze — 5 to 30 minutes per alarm, with a configurable limit on how many times one alarm can be snoozed.
  • Vibrate while ringing — per alarm, with a repeating pattern.
  • Delete after ringing — a one-off alarm can remove itself, or simply switch off and stay in the list.
  • Labels and countdown — every alarm shows its label and "Alarm in 33 minutes" style countdown on the card.
  • Survives reboots — alarms are re-armed after a restart, an app update, a clock change or a timezone change.

Wake-up missions

An alarm can require up to five missions, run in order, before it can be snoozed or dismissed. Each mission is a real interaction with the device — nothing is faked or auto-completed.

  • Math — solve 1–5 arithmetic problems (addition, subtraction, multiplication).
  • Typing — retype a phrase exactly, case-insensitive.
  • Color Tiles — find the one tile whose shade differs, 1–10 times.
  • Arrange Shapes — tap three to five shapes back in the order shown.
  • Find Numbers — pick the target number out of a shuffled grid.
  • Find Alphabets — the same with letters.
  • Memory Tiles — watch a growing light sequence and repeat it.
  • Shake — shake the phone 5–30 times, counted from the accelerometer.
  • Step — walk 10–50 steps, counted by the device's step counter.
  • No dead ends — if a device lacks a sensor or refuses a permission, that mission says so and steps aside instead of trapping you; after three minutes of ringing a skip option appears.

Sleep & snoring

  • Overnight recording — a foreground service listens to the microphone and reads the accelerometer while you sleep.
  • Snore detection — an event is logged when the room stays above an adaptive loudness threshold, with sensitivity in Settings (low / medium / high).
  • Snore clips — the ten seconds around each snore are saved as a WAV you can play back in the morning, or switch clip saving off.
  • Room noise graph — one measured bar per 30 seconds for the whole night.
  • Movement index — how much the phone moved, sampled from the accelerometer.
  • Screen activity — the screen turning off and on is recorded all night: total screen-off time as a sleep proxy, every screen-on moment as a wake-up, unlock count, and a timeline bar showing exactly when the phone was in use.
  • Week strip — the last seven nights, with a dot on the ones you recorded; tap any night for its report.
  • Honest by design — Chronos reports what it measured: snoring, noise and movement. It does not claim to read REM or deep-sleep stages, because a phone cannot measure them.

Alarm wallpaper & sounds

  • Photo behind the ring screen — add up to eight pictures, choose "Random" to rotate one per ring; images are copied into the app's storage so they keep working.
  • Eight bundled tones — Morning Chime, Sunrise Bells, Soft Pulse, Gentle Waves, Classic Bell, Double Beep, Siren Rise and Hard Alarm, grouped by character and previewable in the picker.
  • My music — any audio file on the device, with a preview.
  • Recorded sound — record your own alarm tone with the microphone, then preview, use or delete it.

Design & interaction

  • Next-alarm hero card — the alarm that will actually ring, with a countdown ring that closes as the moment approaches and a live "in 5 hours 12 min" line.
  • Swipe to delete, with undo — swipe an alarm card left; past the threshold it deletes and offers Undo in a snackbar, otherwise it springs back.
  • Long-press an alarm — duplicate it, switch it on or off, or delete it, without opening the editor.
  • Tab transitions — screens slide and fade in the direction you moved through the tabs.
  • Circular timer — the countdown is a ring that empties as time runs out, breathing gently while it runs and animating its digits.
  • Custom controls — spring toggles, a sliding segmented control for theme and sound categories, and press-scale feedback with light haptics on every tappable card (honours the Haptic feedback setting).
  • Ring screen — the clock pulses, a mission progress bar fills as you complete challenges, and each mission cross-fades into the next.
  • World clock — cities carry a day or night tint, and a long-press reorders or removes them.
  • Sleep report — the noise graph and live level meter animate in rather than appearing as static bars.
  • Tabular figures — clock digits are monospaced, so a running timer never makes the layout jump.

World clock

  • Every timezone on the device — the city list comes from Android's own timezone database, grouped alphabetically with a tap-to-jump letter rail.
  • Search — filter by city or region name.
  • Use my location — adds your current timezone in one tap.
  • Offsets and day changes — each city shows its local date, GMT offset difference from you, and marks tomorrow or yesterday.
  • Seconds on or off — controlled from Settings.

Timer

  • Hour / minute / second wheels — a snapping picker, not a text box.
  • Presets — Meeting, Sleep, Exercise and Tea are there to start with; save your own from the current duration and remove the ones you don't use.
  • Runs in the background — a foreground service keeps the countdown alive with a live notification while you use other apps.
  • Accurate through sleep — remaining time is derived from the monotonic clock, so a sleeping device cannot make the timer drift.
  • Add a minute — extend a running countdown without restarting it.
  • Completion alert — tone and vibration when it ends, each toggleable.

Stopwatch

  • Centisecond precision — 00:00.00, with hours appearing automatically past the hour.
  • Laps — split and total for each lap; the fastest and slowest are highlighted once you have two or more.
  • Survives process death — the start instant is persisted, so the count stays correct if the app is backgrounded or restarted.

Privacy and data

  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no ads.
  • Alarms, settings, cities and holidays are stored on your device only.
  • The only network request is the update check against GitHub's public releases API.
  • Diagnostic logs stay on the device until you choose to share or export them.
  • Sleep recordings, snore clips, wallpapers and recorded tones never leave the device.

What is not in this release

Honest scope, so nothing looks bigger than it is. There is no camera-based mission yet — QR/barcode, take-a-photo, push-ups, squats and jumping jacks need a camera pipeline that is not in this build, so they are absent rather than faked. Sleep stages (REM / deep) are not shown because a phone cannot measure them; you get measured snoring, noise and movement instead. Still to come: home-screen widgets, multiple simultaneous timers, and translated languages. These are tracked as the next features rather than half-built placeholders.
